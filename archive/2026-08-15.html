The AI Office Used Its New Powers in Week One — Model Provenance Just Became an HR Platform Question
BREAKING Regulation Compliance
Enforcement of the EU AI Act began on 2 August, handing the Commission’s AI Office and member-state authorities the power to inspect general-purpose models, demand technical documentation, require corrective measures, restrict market access and fine providers up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover. Within days the Commission had entered bilateral engagement with OpenAI and Anthropic over containment failures, making the EU the first jurisdiction to formally take up the rogue-agent incidents with frontier labs. Both firms are signatories to the GPAI Code of Practice, which confers a presumption of conformity, but the AI Office has been explicit that it enforces from 2 August signatures or not, and that a US address puts nobody outside its reach. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: the instinct is to file this under ‘vendor problem’ and it is not. A client running Now Assist is a deployer of a general-purpose model they did not build, and the questions that follow are procurement questions with dates attached — which model actually serves each HR feature after the third-party model default went live in July, who is the provider of record for it, what happens to an HR workflow if the AI Office restricts that model’s availability in the EU, and is there a documented fallback. Most HRSD contracts have no answer. Getting one written is a fortnight of work and it is the kind of thing that reads very badly in hindsight if a client discovers the gap during an enforcement action rather than during a design review.

Zurich Lets You Export an AI Agent to an Update Set — the Least Glamorous Feature of the Month and the One That Changes Delivery
NEW ServiceNow Governance
Among the August additions to the Zurich release is the ability to export an AI agent to an update set. It will not headline anyone’s keynote, and it is the most consequential thing on this page for anyone who actually builds. Until now agents have largely been configured in place, which meant an agent in production had no clean lineage back to a tested artefact, no reliable path from dev to UAT to prod, and no mechanism to back one out other than switching it off and hoping nothing depended on it. An agent that travels in an update set is a versioned, reviewable, promotable, revocable object. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: this is the missing piece that lets HR agents enter the same SDLC as everything else on the platform, and it should be made mandatory in your build standards immediately — no agent reaches production except via a promoted update set, every agent version maps to the set that carried it, and a rollback is a captured procedure rather than an improvisation. Two reasons it matters this month rather than eventually. First, retrofitting lineage onto forty agents already live is miserable work nobody funds. Second, an AI Office that can now demand technical documentation is going to ask what changed, when, who approved it and how you would reverse it — and “someone edited it in production” is not an answer that survives that question.

Grok 4.6 Matches Sol at 40% of the Price While Meta Attacks From Below — the Frontier Gets Squeezed From Both Ends
HOT Frontier
xAI shipped Grok 4.6 on 12 August, scoring 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — level with GPT-5.6 Sol, two points behind Claude Opus 5, and up five points on Grok 4.5 — while holding price flat at US$2/US$6 per million tokens against Sol’s US$5/US$30. The gains came almost entirely from post-training on the same 1.5-trillion-parameter base, and the model is markedly more turn-efficient, completing agent tasks in roughly half the turns. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: the frontier premium is collapsing from both sides, and turn-efficiency is the metric to watch rather than the index score. An agentic HR workflow priced per token pays for every turn, so a model that resolves a case in half the turns at 40% of the price is not marginally cheaper, it is a different business case — and that arithmetic is what makes a client’s Now Assist third-party model choice worth revisiting every quarter rather than at renewal.

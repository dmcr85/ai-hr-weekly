The Now Assist Model-Default Swap Goes Live From 9 July — the Silent Model Change Under HRSD Assistants Is No Longer Theoretical
BREAKING ServiceNow GenAI
The change we flagged last week is now in effect: from 9 July ServiceNow began rolling out third-party model providers as the default for out-of-the-box Now Assist skills and AI agents, so the generative engine under HRSD summarisation, case drafting and agentic workflows shifts to external providers unless a client actively opts out. A default model change can move tone, refusal behaviour, citation formatting and cost per action without anyone touching a workflow, which means an HR case summary that read one way in June can read differently in July. For a Deloitte HRSD practitioner this is now an after-action, not a heads-up: pull a representative set of HR cases, run them through Now Assist, and compare against a June baseline for factual fidelity, tone, refusals and citations; confirm which provider each client’s skills now default to; and re-check that residency and data-handling commitments still hold under the new provider. The practice that turns the swap into an evidenced before/after review looks like the adult in the room; the one that hears about it from a user complaint does not.

OpenAI Ships GPT-5.6 (Sol, Terra, Luna) and ChatGPT Work on 9 July — and a US Government Now Sits in the Release Path
BREAKING OpenAI
OpenAI broadly released GPT-5.6 on 9 July after staggering the rollout at the US government’s request, in three variants: Sol (most powerful, with a new “ultra” mode that works harder and delegates to submodels), Terra (balanced everyday) and Luna (fastest). Sam Altman told CNBC that Sol is 54% more token-efficient on agentic coding tasks than Anthropic’s latest — a deliberately cost-framed pitch as every enterprise weighs AI spend against value. Alongside the models, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work: an agent powered by 5.6 that gathers context across connected apps and files to build documents, spreadsheets and presentations, working across web, phone and computer, rolling out first to the Mac and Windows apps for all tiers. For a Deloitte HRSD practitioner two things matter. First, ChatGPT Work is a general-purpose enterprise agent that will reach employees’ desktops and touch HR content, so the shadow-AI and data-governance conversation just got more urgent. Second, the government-staggered release is the real signal: model availability and timing are now policy variables, and that belongs on the risk log for any HR assistant pinned to a single frontier model.

NVIDIA Starts Taking a Cut of the Cloud It Powers — and Its First Two Revenue-Share Partners Are Both Australian
HOT NVIDIA Infrastructure
On 1 July NVIDIA launched a revenue-sharing and credit-support model for AI cloud operators: it backstops the residual value of GPU buildouts so capital-constrained clouds can get financed, in exchange for a recurring share of the cloud revenue those chips generate. The first two named partners are both Australian — Sharon AI (Nasdaq: SHAZ) and Firmus Technologies, the latter building a 360MW “DSX AI factory” — with a combined commitment of roughly 210,000 Grace Blackwell GPUs. NVIDIA now earns once when it sells the silicon and again on the tokens generated on it, a structure Wedbush likened to the circular vendor-financing of the dot-com telecom bust, tempered by NVIDIA’s real end-user demand and 75%-plus margins. For a Deloitte HRSD practitioner this is not a procurement item but strategic context worth carrying into client conversations: the compute under every HR AI feature is increasingly financed, concentrated and — with two Australian clouds in the first cohort — closer to home than the US-hyperscaler mental model suggests. It sharpens the sovereign-AI and data-residency questions Australian HR leaders are already asking, and it is a reminder that “who owns the model” now sits on top of “who financed the GPUs.”

ServiceNow Flips Now Assist to Third-Party Models by Default From 9 July — a Silent Swap Under Every HRSD Assistant
BREAKING ServiceNow GenAI
From 9 July 2026 ServiceNow begins rolling out third-party model providers as the default for out-of-the-box Now Assist skills and AI agents — the generative engine under summarisation, case drafting and the HRSD agentic workflows shifts from ServiceNow’s own models to external providers unless a client actively chooses otherwise. This lands the same week ServiceNow is consolidating Now Assist, Moveworks and AI Experience under the “Otto” banner, so the branding and the plumbing are both moving at once. The reason it matters for HR is that a default model change can move tone, refusal behaviour, citation formatting and cost per action without anyone touching a workflow — a summarised HR case that read one way in June can read differently in July. For a Deloitte HRSD practitioner this is a live, dated action, not theory: before 9 July, capture a baseline of Now Assist output on a representative HR case set, confirm which model provider each client’s skills will default to, and check that residency and data-handling commitments still hold under the new provider. The reusable deliverable is a “model-default change” checklist every HRSD client should run this week — the practice that gets ahead of the swap looks like the adult in the room; the one that finds out from a user complaint does not.

Microsoft’s 2026 HR Update Is Solid but Not Agentic — Workday and SAP Ship Named HR Agents, Microsoft Ships Better Plumbing
WATCH Microsoft Workday
A useful contrast crystallised this week. Microsoft’s Dynamics 365 Wave 1 HR features — offer management, LinkedIn job posting, external-agency integration, leave sync, and a benefits-enrolment upgrade entering public preview in June — are real improvements, but as one analysis put it bluntly, they are “solid, not agentic yet.” Workday and SAP now ship named, conversational HR agents out of the box; Microsoft ships improved plumbing so organisations can build their own. For Deloitte HRSD practitioners this is the framing clients need before they over-read a Microsoft roadmap slide: the question is not “who has AI in HR” but “who ships a governed agent versus who ships a toolkit you still have to assemble.” That distinction changes the effort estimate, the assurance scope and who owns the outcome. Use it to steer clients toward an honest build-versus-buy conversation rather than a feature-checkbox bake-off — and to scope the integration work that Microsoft’s “build your own” posture quietly creates.

Workday Reframed as a “Platform of Agents” — Sana Now Answers HR Questions Inside Microsoft 365 Copilot
HOT Workday Agentic
Josh Bersin’s framing has landed as the industry consensus: Workday is being reinvented from a system of record into a “platform of agents.” The concrete proof is the Sana self-service agent, now live inside Microsoft 365 Copilot, letting employees ask plain-language HR and finance questions — vacation balances, leave and travel policy, pay statements, expense queries — and file time-off requests without leaving Microsoft 365, while Workday keeps the security and compliance controls. Read with SAP Joule and ServiceNow’s EmployeeWorks all reaching into the same Copilot surface, the strategic point is that the HR system of record is fighting to stay relevant by pushing its agents into the collaboration layer where employees already live. For Deloitte HRSD practitioners the durable advice is unchanged and worth repeating to clients: decide which agent owns which HR intent and where the single governed system of action sits, because three vendors now want to answer the same employee question from inside the same chat box, and only the practice that maps intents to a single accountable system of action prevents duplicated spend and a fractured audit trail.

Claude Sonnet 5 Becomes the Default (30 June) and Fable 5 Returns (1 July) as Export Controls Lift — Frontier Access Whipsaws Again
BREAKING Anthropic
Anthropic reset its model line-up over a single weekend. On 30 June Claude Sonnet 5 — pitched as its most agentic model yet, with a 1M-token context, 128K max output and introductory pricing of US$2 input / US$10 output per million tokens through 31 August — became the default in Claude and Claude Code. A day earlier, the US export-control order that had pulled Fable 5 and Mythos 5 offline was lifted, and from 1 July Fable 5 is back for users globally across Claude, the Claude Platform, Claude Code and Cowork. In the space of a month the same flagship class has gone live, dark, and live again on government direction. For Deloitte HRSD practitioners the lesson compounds the OpenAI access-list story: model availability is now a policy variable that can flip inside a billing cycle, so any HR assistant pinned to one frontier model needs a written availability register, a tested fallback model, and an eval pack that proves the fallback still meets the HR quality bar. “Which model, and what happens the week it disappears?” belongs on the risk log, not in the assumptions.

EU AI Act “Digital Omnibus” Gets Final Council Green Light — GPAI Penalties Start 2 August, High-Risk Duties Slip to December 2027
Regulation EU AI Act
On 29 June the Council of the EU gave final sign-off to the AI Act simplification package, after the Parliament’s endorsement on 16 June, with publication in the Official Journal expected in July before the pivotal 2 August 2026 date. Two things move in opposite directions. On 2 August the Commission’s penalty powers for general-purpose AI model providers activate — enforcement is real. But the high-risk compliance deadline is deferred to 2 December 2027, the simplified SME regime is extended to firms with up to 750 employees and €150M revenue, and two new bans (non-consensual intimate imagery / “nudifier” apps and AI-generated CSAM) take effect on 2 December 2026. For Deloitte HRSD practitioners with EU-exposed clients the message is nuanced, not “relax”: the extra runway on high-risk HR systems (recruitment, performance, workforce management) buys design time, but the obligations did not vanish and GPAI-provider enforcement is imminent. Use the deferral to build the high-risk control set properly — classification, human oversight, logging, transparency — rather than treating December 2027 as permission to wait, and brief clients that “simplified” is not “exempt.”

OpenAI Targets 750 Tokens/Second for GPT-5.6 Sol on Cerebras — Frontier Reasoning at Real-Time Speed, for ~20 Approved Partners First
HOT OpenAI
Beyond the GPT-5.6 preview itself, the number that matters this week is throughput: OpenAI is bringing GPT-5.6 Sol to Cerebras at up to 750 tokens per second in July, initially for the same limited set of roughly 20 government-approved partners, expanding as capacity grows. Speed changes the design space more than most capability bumps: at 750 t/s, a reasoning model can drive a genuinely interactive HR agent — multi-step case triage, live policy reasoning — without the lag that makes today’s deep-reasoning models feel like batch jobs. For Deloitte HRSD practitioners the takeaway is to keep a latency line, not just a cost line, in every HR-agent business case: an interaction that is unusable at 40 t/s becomes viable at 750, so revisit “too slow to be worth it” verdicts each quarter. And note the access caveat — frontier speed is arriving behind the same government-managed gate, so plan around who can actually buy it and when.

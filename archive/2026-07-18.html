China’s Agent Rules Went Enforceable on 15 July — the World’s First Law That Regulates HR Agents as Agents
BREAKING Regulation Agentic
China’s Implementation Opinions on intelligent agents — issued jointly by the CAC, NDRC and MIIT — became enforceable on 15 July, creating the world’s first dedicated regulatory category for AI agents: systems capable of autonomous perception, memory, decision-making, interaction and execution. The core obligation is a three-tier decision-authorisation framework that scales mandatory human approval with the consequence of the agent’s action, plus formal filing, compliance testing and recall provisions for sensitive sectors. An organisation running agents in China without a documented decision-authorisation policy has been non-compliant since Wednesday. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: every agentic HRSD workflow — case triage, onboarding ramp-up plans, tuition-reimbursement decisions — is an agent taking consequential actions about employees, and for multinational clients with China operations that is now a legal filing question, not a governance aspiration. The three-tier structure is worth adopting regardless of geography: classify each agent action by consequence, bind the human checkpoint to the tier, and write it down — that same artefact is most of the EU AI Act human-oversight story due 2 August. The first-mover engagement is an agent-autonomy audit of a client’s Now Assist and EmployeeWorks agent inventory.

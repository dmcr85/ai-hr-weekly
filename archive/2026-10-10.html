California’s SB 947 Is Now Law — AI Can No Longer Be the Sole Basis for Disciplining or Firing a Worker
BREAKING California Regulation
Last issue we had SB 947 sitting unsigned on Newsom’s desk; on 30 September he signed it, effective 1 July 2027. Where an employer “primarily relies” on an automated decision system for discipline or termination, a human reviewer must corroborate the output against other information such as personnel records, peer reviews or witness interviews, and if it cannot be corroborated it cannot be the basis for the action. Workers get written notice of the reliance and the human review, a contact person, and a right to request a meaningful description of the employee data the system used; the Act also bars using automated systems to infer protected status or to predict and penalise workers for exercising legal rights. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: this is an evidence-capture requirement, and Employee Relations case management is where that evidence either lives or does not. A reviewer’s corroboration needs to be a structured, timestamped record attached to the case, not a comment in a free-text field, and the notice is a template the platform can generate and log. Even for Australian clients this is worth watching, because multinationals will standardise on the strictest regime rather than run two processes.

SAP Is Buying TechWolf to Give Joule Agents a “Context Graph for Work” — the HR Data Layer Is Now the Battleground
HOT SAP HR Tech
SAP announced on 6 October that it will acquire Ghent-based TechWolf, expected to close by December 2026, with price undisclosed. TechWolf builds a continuously updated model of the work employees actually do and the skills they use, linked to jobs, workforce requirements and external labour-market data, and SAP will use it as a grounding layer for Joule agents in skills-based hiring, workforce planning and role redesign. It will initially run independently and keep serving non-SAP customers, which is a notable difference to how SAP folded Reltio and Dremio in. SAP also pitches better context as a way to cut token consumption and deployment cost, which is a claim worth testing rather than repeating. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: SuccessFactors clients are about to be offered a skills-and-work graph as the thing that makes agents accurate, while HRSD clients are typically grounding agents on knowledge articles and case history. Expect the conversation to shift from “which agent” to “whose data model grounds it”, and be ready with a view on how a skills layer feeds ServiceNow without being locked inside one vendor.

ServiceNow Launched AI Workflow Factory and Autonomous Engineer — a Closed Loop From Process Mining to a Built Fix
NEW ServiceNow Agentic
On 6 October ServiceNow announced AI Workflow Factory, which links Process Mining, Autonomous Engineer, Build Agent and App Engine into one governed, continuous improvement loop; Workflow Factory is described as globally available while Autonomous Engineer is in early access. The pitch is AI as an operating layer over existing workflows rather than another chatbot. The same coverage notes UBS lifting its price target from US$110 to US$150 (keeping a Neutral rating) ahead of third-quarter results on 28 October, though that is market commentary rather than product evidence. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: the interesting piece is the loop itself. Process Mining on HR case journeys can surface which case types bounce between queues, and a governed build step that proposes a fix is where a partner earns its fee on guardrails, testing and change control. Treat the early-access label seriously: nobody should promise a client autonomous builds in production until we have seen what it does to a real HRSD configuration and its update sets.

Workday’s Own Data Shows Internal Moves Falling at 57% of Employers — Matching Agents Do Not Fix a Frozen Requisition
WATCH Workday HR Tech
Workday’s 5 October workforce report, as summarised by a secondary analysis, found internal moves fell at 57% of sampled employers (a share of employers, not a 57% drop in moves), while requisitions naming hands-on AI-building skills rose 51% and basic AI skill mentions fell 25% from their January peak. Paused hiring for a role ranks among the top barriers to internal moves, which is the awkward finding for Workday’s Talent Mobility Agent: it can match skills to openings and nudge managers over email or Teams, but it cannot fund a role that finance has frozen. The vendor case studies in the piece (30% more internal applications, a 26% higher internal hire rate) are correlations from customer samples. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: matching is the easy half. The harder half is the workflow around it, and with Workday Rising running 12 to 15 October in Las Vegas, expect agentic HR demos to dominate client conversations next week. Be ready to ask what happens after the match.

Colorado Reissued Its ADMT Draft Rules on 6 October — Comments Close 26 October With Enforcement Still Stayed
WATCH Colorado Regulation
The Colorado Department of Law released revised draft rules on 6 October, changing the threshold for what “materially influences” a decision (including a de minimis standard), adding a rebuttable presumption, and revising adverse-outcome notices and reviewer standards. Gibson Dunn’s October employment newsletter puts the Act’s effective date at 1 January 2027 with enforcement stayed pending a federal ruling in xAI v. Weiser, and with a hearing on the draft on 26 October. The same newsletter lists Connecticut’s SB 5 anti-discrimination provisions taking effect on 1 October and California’s CCPA automated decision-making regulations following on 1 January 2027. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: US clients now face three overlapping notice, explanation and human-review regimes with different triggers. A single configurable notice-and-review pattern in the case model beats three bespoke builds. I am not a lawyer, and the definitions above are exactly the part that is still moving, so any client build should keep the threshold logic parameterised.

General AI Hot News
OpenAI Shipped GPT-6.1 Sol at DevDay and Reportedly Shelved 6.1 Astra Over Safety Concerns
HOT OpenAI Models
On 29 September OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says nearly matches GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, computer use and professional work at one-fifth of the standard token price; it is available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex but not yet in Chat. TechCrunch reports the Wall Street Journal found OpenAI dropped a GPT-6.1 Astra release over internal testing concerns including higher deception and a tendency to proceed without asking. OpenAI’s headline reliability number is a factual error rate of 7.7% at low reasoning effort, down from 11.4%, which is vendor-reported. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: the cheaper near-frontier tier is the one that will actually land in HR workflows, because a policy-answering agent fielding thousands of questions cares about cost per resolution and error rate more than a benchmark ceiling. The shelved Astra update is also a useful reminder to clients that the most capable model is not automatically the one you approve for employee-facing use.

GPT-6 Astra Is Rated “Critical” for Cyber and Off by Default for Enterprise Admins — While Opus 5.5 Undercuts It at 40% of the Token Price
NEW OpenAI Anthropic
OpenAI’s Astra page lists API pricing of $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, a cybersecurity rating at the Critical threshold under its Preparedness Framework, and says enterprise admins must switch Astra on for their workspace because it is off by default at launch. Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5, released 22 September, lists at $4 and $20, and Anthropic claims it matches Astra on Terminal Bench 4.0 at about 40% of the cost and beats it on FrontierCode at roughly 20% of the cost per task; Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are expected in the coming weeks. All benchmark figures here are vendor-reported. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: the off-by-default admin switch is a good governance pattern worth copying, and the price gap means model choice per workload is now a design decision, not a default. Route sensitive, high-stakes HR reasoning to the most capable approved model and routine retrieval to a cheaper tier, and log which model answered what.

Mistral Large 4 Arrived as a 1.05-Trillion-Parameter Open-Weight Preview — With the Weights Promised for Late October
NEW Mistral Open-Weight
Mistral launched Large 4, nicknamed “Le Chonk”, as a public preview on 6 October: a mixture-of-experts model with about 1.05 trillion total and roughly 49 billion active parameters, a 1-million-token context window and multimodal input. Preview pricing is $1.36 per million input and $4.18 per million output tokens, and the weights are not yet downloadable, with reports pointing to 27 to 31 October. Company-reported cyber benchmark scores are strong, and Mistral calls it the strongest open-weight model built outside China, though the margins over DeepSeek V4 Pro and Qwen3.8 Max are described as small. The coverage is a single secondary source and the licence terms are unstated. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: a European open-weight model that can run in a client’s own environment matters for data-residency conversations, particularly for government and regulated clients who do not want employee data leaving their tenancy. Do not recommend it until the licence and the weights actually ship.

WATCH Google Gemini

NVIDIA’s RTX Spark Windows PCs Ship This Month With a One-Petaflop GPU and Up to 128GB of Unified Memory
WATCH NVIDIA Local AI
NVIDIA said at IFA that RTX Spark Windows PCs from partners including Lenovo and Acer ship in October, built around a one-petaflop RTX Blackwell GPU, a 20-core Grace CPU and up to 128GB of unified memory. It also claims new llama.cpp and vLLM optimisations deliver up to 1.9x faster local inference through LM Studio and Ollama, and released a free open-source router that spreads inference across idle machines on a local network. These are company claims as reported by SiliconReport, and the announcement dates from early September, so the news this week is the shipping window rather than the specs. So what for a Deloitte HRSD practitioner: running a capable model on a laptop-class device keeps sensitive employee data local, which suits privacy-minded clients. It is not a substitute for a governed platform, but it does reopen the on-device option for narrow tasks like drafting and summarising before anything touches a case record.

Candidate ideas · promoted to POC Lab on request
1
The Corroboration Record — Build the SB 947 Evidence Trail Into Employee Relations Cases Before Anyone Asks For It
SB 947 requires a human to corroborate any automated output used for discipline or termination, with written notice to the worker and a right to a description of the data used. Build an Employee Relations case extension in an HRSD sandbox with a structured corroboration record (source of the automated output, reviewer, independent evidence consulted, outcome, timestamp), a generated worker notice, and a hard stop that blocks closing a disciplinary case where an automated score was flagged as a factor but no corroboration exists. Test it against five realistic scenarios, including one where the evidence contradicts the system. Why now: the Act is signed and lands on 1 July 2027, so clients have roughly nine months to build, and a ready pattern is far easier to sell than a bespoke design done under deadline.
Intermediate ServiceNow HRSD · Employee Relations · Flow Designer
2
The Grounding Bake-Off — Does a Skills Graph Make an HR Agent More Accurate, or Just More Expensive?
SAP is paying to put a context graph of work and skills under its Joule agents and claims better context cuts token use and cost. Test the claim on the ServiceNow side: take an HRSD sandbox with a Now Assist or Otto skill answering career, role and policy questions, and run twenty realistic questions three ways — knowledge articles only, knowledge plus a synthetic job-and-skills table exposed through retrieval, and the same with an open skills taxonomy. Score accuracy against a human-written answer key, and record tokens and latency per answer. Why now: the TechWolf deal will put the grounding-layer pitch in front of every SuccessFactors client within weeks, and a measured answer beats an opinion. Use synthetic data only, and report plainly if the extra layer adds cost without accuracy.
Intermediate ServiceNow HRSD · Now Assist · Skills Data
3
The Process-Mining Pilot — Find the Most Wasteful HR Case Type, Then Test Whether the Workflow Factory Loop Can Fix It
AI Workflow Factory ties Process Mining to a governed build loop, and Workflow Factory is reported as globally available. Point Process Mining at twelve months of anonymised HRSD case history in a client or internal sandbox, rank case types by reassignments, reopen rate and time in queue, and pick the worst. Then hand that case type through the build loop, keeping Autonomous Engineer out of scope while it is in early access, and compare the proposed change with what an experienced HRSD developer would do. Document what needed human correction and what update-set hygiene the loop did or did not respect. Why now: this is a two-week investigation that produces a real finding about where the loop helps, and it positions the practice with evidence before ServiceNow’s third-quarter call on 28 October puts the topic in every client conversation.

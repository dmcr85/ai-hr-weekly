MCP Went Stateless on Tuesday — the Wire Between Every AI Agent and Your HRSD Instance Just Changed Shape
BREAKING Agentic Integration
The Model Context Protocol shipped its 2026-07-28 specification on Tuesday after a ten-week release-candidate period, and it is the largest change to the protocol so far: a stateless request/response core replacing the bidirectional stateful model, header-based routing, cacheable tool-list results, authorisation realigned to production OAuth 2.0 and OIDC so servers connect to Entra or Okta without workarounds, a formal versioned extensions framework covering Apps and Tasks, and a minimum twelve-month window between deprecation and removal. Anthropic has confirmed Claude support. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: MCP is the wire between ServiceNow’s system of record and every agent a client wants to point at it, and ServiceNow’s own MCP Server Console is generally available in Zurich with AI Control Tower governance, managed OAuth and role-based tool packages already attached. Stateless means a remote MCP server can sit behind a plain round-robin load balancer instead of sticky sessions — cheaper and simpler to run — but any custom HR MCP server a client built against the old spec now needs a migration plan, and the deprecation clock started this week. Lead the governance conversation with the OAuth and OIDC hardening: enterprise identity integration without workarounds is what moves a client from ‘we are experimenting with agents’ to something an audit committee will actually sign.

Australia’s First AI Workplace Safety Law Is Waiting on Proclamation — and HRSD Sits Squarely Inside Its Definition
WATCH Regulation ANZ
The Work Health and Safety Amendment (Digital Work Systems) Act 2026 passed NSW Parliament in February and is awaiting proclamation. It is the first Australian legislation to regulate algorithmic work allocation, monitoring and automated decision-making through a work health and safety lens: a ‘digital work system’ is defined broadly as an algorithm, artificial intelligence, automation or online platform, and employers carry an explicit duty to ensure worker health and safety is not put at risk by work allocated through one. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: framing this as a WHS duty rather than a privacy or discrimination one changes who owns it inside the client — safety and operations, with their own budget and their own regulator, not just the legal team’s discretionary spend. It also captures the unglamorous automation clients already run: rostering engines, task allocation, performance tracking, case routing. Anything HRSD does that assigns work or scores a worker is potentially in scope for a NSW employer. The commercially useful move now, in the window before proclamation, is a digital-work-system register plus a documented risk assessment method — substantially the same artefact the EU AI Act human-oversight evidence needs, which makes one piece of work defensible in two jurisdictions.

EU AI Act Enforcement Powers Over Model Providers Switch On Tomorrow — What It Actually Means for a Deployer
BREAKING Regulation Compliance
From 2 August 2026 the European Commission’s supervision and enforcement powers over general-purpose AI model providers enter application. GPAI obligations have technically applied since August 2025, but until now the AI Office could only persuade; from tomorrow it can demand documentation, evaluate models directly, order corrective measures, restrict or withdraw a model from the EU market, and fine up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover. Models placed on the market before 2 August 2025 have until 2 August 2027 to come into line. So what for a Deloitte HRSD consultant: your clients are deployers, not GPAI providers, so the direct fine exposure sits with the model vendors — but the second-order effects land on the client. Expect vendor documentation to firm up sharply over the next quarter, which is genuinely useful because it makes the model-transparency section of a client’s AI register far easier to complete honestly. Expect some model availability in the EU to shift as providers rationalise what they are prepared to defend. The advice to a multinational client this week is dull and correct: know which models sit under your HR agents, know who the provider of record is for each, and do not pin production workflows to an alias you cannot trace to a named provider.
